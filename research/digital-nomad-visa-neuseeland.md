## Key message
New Zealand has NO dedicated "Digital Nomad Visa" programme. Since 27 January 2025, however, holders of an ordinary visitor visa or an NZeTA (visa waiver) are explicitly allowed to work remotely for a foreign employer/client — a policy change that Immigration New Zealand expressly promotes as "digital nomad"-friendly. There is NO German-New Zealand social security agreement (Sozialversicherungsabkommen) (unlike Canada/USA/Brazil) — New Zealand is entirely absent from the official list of agreement states of Deutsche Rentenversicherung (German pension insurance, DRV). For tax, a 92-day exemption threshold applies without double taxation agreement protection, or up to 183 days with DBA protection (Germany has a double taxation agreement (DBA) with New Zealand); beyond that, NZ tax liability from the first day of the stay.

## Sources (all retrieved and read, 2026-09-04)

### 1. Immigration New Zealand — "Working remotely in New Zealand on a visitor visa" (official, immigration.govt.nz)
URL: https://www.immigration.govt.nz/visit/checking-or-changing-the-conditions-of-your-visitor-visa-or-nzeta/working-remotely-in-new-zealand-on-a-visitor-visa/
Quote: "You may be able to work remotely for an overseas business or client while you are visiting New Zealand. Remote work is an activity you do for gain or reward for a company, employer or client that is not in New Zealand." and "If you are self-employed or a digital nomad you can work remotely for clients outside New Zealand." and "All visitor visas applied for on or after 27 January 2025 allow you to work remotely in New Zealand. There is no limit to the amount of remote work you can do while you have your visitor visa."
Exclusions: "Remote work does not include any work you do: that is for a New Zealand employer; with a New Zealand business or person in New Zealand in exchange for goods or services...; that requires you to be in New Zealand."
Tax note on the same page: "If your income is taxed in another country or territory, you may not need to pay tax in New Zealand if you are here for less than 92 days in a 12-month period. If you are a tax resident in 1 of the countries... New Zealand has a tax treaty with, you may be able to stay in New Zealand for up to 183 days before you need to pay tax."
→ No dedicated DNV product, but a change to the visa conditions of the existing visitor visa/NZeTA; explicitly "no limit" for remote work during the visa period, but strict demarcation from NZ employers/clients.

### 2. Immigration New Zealand — news release "Working remotely from New Zealand" (official, 27.01.2025)
URL: https://www.immigration.govt.nz/about-us/news-centre/working-remotely-from-new-zealand/
Quote: "The Government announced today that the rules for visitors are changing and people arriving on a visitor visa or NZeTA can work for overseas employers while visiting New Zealand." and "These new conditions mean that tourists can stay in New Zealand as a digital nomad and keep in touch with work back home, without breaching their visa conditions." and "Both visitor visa recipients and people who enter with an NZeTA (New Zealand Electronic Travel Authority) will receive these conditions."
Tax detail (officially quoted): "Generally, if the person's income is taxed elsewhere, New Zealand will exempt it from tax if the person does not spend more than 92 days in New Zealand in a 12-month period... If the person is tax resident in one of the 40 plus countries with which New Zealand has a tax treaty (such as Australia, most of Europe, the UK, the US and much of Asia), this period can often be extended to 183 days."
→ Confirms the date of the policy change (27.01.2025), applies to both the visitor visa and the NZeTA, and that Germany as a DBA country (part of "most of Europe") potentially benefits from the 183-day extension instead of only 92 days.

### 3. Inland Revenue NZ — "Tax residency status for individuals" (official, ird.govt.nz)
URL: https://www.ird.govt.nz/international-tax/individuals/tax-residency-status-for-individuals
Quote: "You become a New Zealand tax resident when the first of these happens: you've been in New Zealand for more than 183 days in any 12-month period (unless you're a non-resident visitor); you have a permanent place of abode in New Zealand." and "Parts of days (such as the day you arrive and leave) count as whole days towards the 183 days. The 183 days do not need to follow each other. Your New Zealand tax residency status is backdated to the first of the 183 days."
Non-resident visitor exception: "You're a non-resident visitor if you visit New Zealand for up to 275 days total in any 18-month period, and: you're not working for or paid by a New Zealand resident... you're required to pay taxes in a country where you're a tax resident" (among others).
→ Official 183-day rule (analogous to the German approach, but NZ-specific: days need not be consecutive, backdating to day 1). For the 92-day threshold without DBA protection, IRD refers to the Immigration NZ page (Source 1/2) and tax treaty information.

### 4. Deutsche Rentenversicherung — "Germany's social security agreements" (Deutschlands Sozialversicherungsabkommen) (official, complete country list)
URL: https://www.deutsche-rentenversicherung.de/DRV/DE/Rente/Ausland/Sozialversicherungsabkommen/sozialversicherungsabkommen_detailseite.html
Quote (translated from German): "Bilateral social security agreements currently apply in relation to the following states..." — the complete list names: Albania, Australia, Bosnia-Herzegovina, Brazil, Chile, India, Israel, Japan, Canada and Quebec, Kosovo, Morocco, Moldova, Montenegro, North Macedonia, Philippines, Serbia, South Korea, Tunisia, Turkey, Uruguay, USA (plus a separate posting agreement with China). **New Zealand does not appear in either list.**
→ Primary source that there is no German-New Zealand social security agreement (unlike the countries already covered: Canada, USA, Brazil, India). For German remote workers in New Zealand, therefore, only the national law of both states applies (possibly Ausstrahlung under section 4 of the Social Code Book IV (§ 4 SGB IV — continued application of German social security law during temporary work abroad) in the case of a formal posting (Entsendung), otherwise no automatic coverage).

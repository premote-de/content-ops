## Sources (all fetched/read in this session)

### 1. Ministério das Relações Exteriores — Consulado-Geral do Brasil em Lisboa: VITEM XIV
https://www.gov.br/mre/pt-br/consulado-lisboa/servicos-consulares/vistos-destinados-a-estrangeiros-para-entrada-no-brasil/visto-temporario-xiv-2013-nomade-digital-vitem-xiv
(live page shows CAPTCHA protection; content verified via Wayback snapshot 2025-12-04 — official
gov.br domain, rules unchanged since the 2021 CNIg resolution, see Source 2)
Quote (translated from Portuguese): "The applicant for this type of visa must prove means of subsistence from a foreign paying source in a monthly amount equal to or higher than US$ 1,500.00 (one thousand five hundred US dollars), or the availability of bank funds of at least US$ 18,000.00 (eighteen thousand US dollars). The VITEM XIV will have an initial residence period of up to one (1) year." Documents required for the digital-nomad proof: (1) signed declaration of the
ability to work remotely, (2) employment or service contract with a foreign employer,
(3) proof of income/assets as above. After entry: 90-day deadline to register with the
Policía Federal.

### 2. Conselho Nacional de Imigração (CNIg) — Resolução CNIg MJSP Nº 45, de 9 de setembro de 2021
https://www.in.gov.br/en/web/dou/-/resolucao-cnig-mjsp-n-45-de-9-de-setembro-de-2021-375554693
(Diário Oficial da União, via Wayback snapshot 2024-04-06, official legal gazette publication)
Legal basis of the VITEM XIV. Quote Art. 1 §1 (translated from Portuguese): "a 'digital nomad' is an immigrant who, remotely and using information and communication technologies, is able to carry out their work activities in Brazil for a foreign employer." §2 explicitly excludes
persons with a Brazilian employer. Art. 3 Par. único and Art. 4 Par. único: (translated) "The initial residence period ... will be up to one year." Art. 6: extension for the same period possible
upon renewed submission of the documents.

### 3. Receita Federal do Brasil — "Residente e Não Residente"
https://www.gov.br/receitafederal/pt-br/assuntos/meu-imposto-de-renda/preenchimento/dsdp/nao-residente
(retrieved live, official tax authority, updated 21.10.2025)
Quote (non-resident, translated from Portuguese): "[A person is considered non-resident who] enters Brazil on a temporary visa and stays up to 183 days, consecutive or not, within a period of up to 12 months." Quote (resident, translated): "[A person is considered resident who] enters Brazil on a temporary visa: ... b) on the date on which they complete 184 days, consecutive or not, of stay in Brazil within a period of up to twelve months." → Key fact: the threshold is 184 days (not 183) — tax residency begins on the
184th day; up to the 183rd day the status remains non-resident. Applies regardless of
VITEM XIV status; the visa duration (up to 1 year) and the tax threshold (184 days) are legally
separate concepts.

### 4. Deutsche Rentenversicherung Bund (German federal pension insurance, DRV Bund) — "Application forms for posting certificates based on

https://www.deutsche-rentenversicherung.de/DRV/DE/Rente/Ausland/Ansprechpartner-und-Verbindungsstellen/entsendebescheinigung
(retrieved live, official German social security authority)
Confirmed: Germany has a bilateral social security agreement with Brazil. The competence
table lists "Brasilien" with form code "BR/DE 101" (questionnaire for the issue of the

(not DVKA), provided that no pension insurance contributions are paid to a health insurance fund

Form PDF: https://www.deutsche-rentenversicherung.de/SharedDocs/Downloads/DE/Rente/

## Key messages for the article
1. Brazil has NO "Digital Nomad Visa" by brand name — officially it is called "Visto Temporário
   XIV — Nômade Digital (VITEM XIV)", governed by CNIg Resolution 45/2021.
2. Requirements: monthly foreign income ≥ USD 1,500 OR bank balance ≥ USD 18,000, contract
   with a foreign employer, no Brazilian clients/employer. Duration: up to 1 year,
   extendable by a further 12 months.
3. Tax: non-resident up to 183 days, from the 184th day (not the 183rd) automatically resident with
   worldwide income tax liability in Brazil — the difference from the "183-day" convention in other
   countries (USA IRS Substantial Presence Test, Canada CRA sojourner) is the day threshold for the
   change of status (184th instead of 183rd day).
4. German social security: as a rule continues via section 4 SGB IV (Ausstrahlung), evidenced
   NOT via A1 (applies only to EU/EEA/CH), but via the bilateral German-Brazilian social security agreement,
   form BR/DE 101, competent body DRV Bund.
5. Important distinction: the visa itself only governs residence status — neither German social security liability
   nor Brazilian tax liability is automatically triggered or removed by it.

## For the comparison with other countries (taken from existing articles, not newly researched)
- USA: Certificate of Coverage D/USA 101, DVKA responsible (a1-bescheinigung-usa)
- Canada: separate social security agreement, DRV Bund/DVKA (digital-nomad-visa-kanada)
- Malaysia: NO social security agreement with Germany — pure Ausstrahlung case without a posting certificate
  (digital-nomad-visa-malaysia)

## Core question of the article
What are the legal rules when employees of a German company temporarily work from Greece (workation)? Is an A1 certificate (A1-Bescheinigung) required, how does entry work for EU citizens, and when does a workation tip over into Greek taxation?

## Facts + sources (all retrieved and read first-hand, 2026-09-10)

### 1. The A1 certificate applies to Greece as an EU Member State (standard case, no special regime)
Source: Deutsche Rentenversicherung (German pension insurance, DRV), FAQ "A1-Bescheinigung – Arbeiten im EU-Ausland" (A1 certificate – working in other EU countries)
URL: https://www.deutsche-rentenversicherung.de/SharedDocs/FAQ/a1_bescheinigung/a1_bescheinigung_faq_liste
Retrieved: 2026-09-10

Quotes (translated from the German original): "As a rule, all persons are subject to the social security legislation of the state in which they are gainfully employed. If these persons work only temporarily in another Member State of the European Union (EU), in Iceland, Liechtenstein, Norway, Switzerland or in the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Northern Ireland (so-called posting), the law of the sending state exceptionally continues to apply." And: "The A1 certificate is issued for cross-border gainful activities in the EU Member States as well as in Iceland, Liechtenstein, Norway, Switzerland and the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Northern Ireland."
→ Greece is an EU Member State and therefore falls within the territorial scope of the A1 certificate. No special Greek agreement is needed — the normal EU coordination rules (Regulation (EC) No 883/2004) apply, as for any other EU workation. An A1 is advisable/required whenever the work takes place abroad, regardless of whether it is a classic posting (Entsendung) or a workation.

### 2. Germany–Greece double taxation agreement (DBA): 183-day rule for wage tax (Article XI)
Source: Federal Ministry of Finance (Bundesministerium der Finanzen, BMF), Agreement between the Federal Republic of Germany and the Kingdom of Greece for the avoidance of double taxation (18 April 1966, in force since 8 December 1967, BGBl. 1967 II p. 852)
URL (overview page): https://www.bundesfinanzministerium.de/Content/DE/Standardartikel/Themen/Steuern/Internationales_Steuerrecht/Staatenbezogene_Informationen/Laender_A_Z/Griechenland/1967-02-25-Griechenland-Abkommen-DBA.html
URL (full-text PDF): https://www.bundesfinanzministerium.de/Content/DE/Standardartikel/Themen/Steuern/Internationales_Steuerrecht/Staatenbezogene_Informationen/Laender_A_Z/Griechenland/1967-02-25-Griechenland-Abkommen-DBA-Gesetz.pdf?__blob=publicationFile&v=3
Retrieved: 2026-09-10 (PDF extracted via pdf-parse, since the full text is not available as HTML)

Quote (Article XI paras. 2-3, translated from German): "(2) Salaries, wages and similar remuneration derived by a resident of a Contracting State in respect of dependent employment shall be taxable only in that State unless the employment is exercised in the other Contracting State. If the employment is so exercised, such remuneration as is derived therefrom may be taxed in that other State. (3) Notwithstanding paragraph 2, remuneration derived by a resident of a Contracting State in respect of dependent employment exercised in the other Contracting State shall be taxable only in the first-mentioned State if a) the recipient is present in the other State for a period or periods not exceeding in the aggregate 183 days in the tax year concerned, b) the remuneration is paid by, or on behalf of, an employer who is not a resident of the other State, and c) the remuneration is not deducted from the profits of a permanent establishment or a fixed base which the employer has in the other State."
→ Classic 183-day rule: as long as the employee does not spend more than 183 days in Greece in a tax year, the employer remains resident in Germany (not in Greece) and the remuneration is not borne by a Greek permanent establishment (Betriebsstätte)/fixed base, Germany retains the right to tax the employment income. All three conditions must be met cumulatively — if even one condition is not met, Greece has the right to tax from day 1.

### 3. EU freedom of movement: German nationals can stay in Greece visa-free for 3 months, after which registration is required
Source: European Commission, "Your Europe" – "Registering residence abroad after the first 3 months"
URL: https://europa.eu/youreurope/citizens/residence/documents-formalities/registering-residence/index_en.htm
Retrieved: 2026-09-10

Quote: "During the first 3 months of your stay in your new country, as EU national, you cannot be required to apply for a residence document confirming your right to live there — although in some countries you may have to report your presence upon arrival. After 3 months in your new country, you may be required to register your residence with the relevant authority (often the town hall or local police station), and to be issued with a registration certificate." Requirement for employees: "Certificate of employment or confirmation of recruitment from your employer."
→ No visa is needed for entry as a German/EU national (freedom of movement). For a workation extending beyond 3 months, Greece may require reporting/registration with a registration certificate — as an EU citizen, however, the right of residence always exists regardless of registration (a fine is possible, but no expulsion merely for failing to register, according to the same source).

### 4. EU definition of "posted worker": a workation is typically NOT a posting within the meaning of the PWD
Source: European Commission, Employment, Social Affairs & Inclusion – "Posted workers"
URL: https://employment-social-affairs.ec.europa.eu/policies-and-activities/moving-working-europe/working-another-eu-country/posted-workers_en
Retrieved: 2026-09-10

Quote: "A 'posted worker' is an employee who is sent by his employer to carry out a service in another EU Member State on a temporary basis, in the context of a contract of services, an intra-group posting or a hiring out through a temporary agency." Legal basis: Posting of Workers Directive (PWD), 1996, revised 2018.
→ The decisive factor is the link to a contract/service: a posting (Entsendung) within the meaning of the PWD requires that the employee is sent by the employer to provide a specific service in the destination country (contract for services, intra-group posting, temporary agency work). A workation — where the employee, on their own initiative and for private reasons, continues to work for the German employer from Greece without providing a specific service in Greece — as a rule does NOT meet this definition. As a result, the PWD notification obligation (minimum wage, notification portal, etc.) that applies to classic postings to Greece generally does not apply. For the genuine posting case (service/contract for services/construction site in Greece), the article refers to the existing article `entsendung-meldung-griechenland` (Greek Ministry of Labour YPAKP, E-SERVICES portal), which covers the notification obligation for that case in detail.

## Implications for the article
Key messages for the DE/EN article:
1. Greece is an EU Member State — the A1 certificate under Regulation (EC) No 883/2004 is the standard proof, no special procedure needed (Source 1).
2. Entry for German employees is visa-free (EU freedom of movement); from day 91 a registration requirement may apply (Source 3).
3. For tax purposes Germany remains competent as long as all three conditions of Art. XI para. 3 DBA are met: max. 183 days/tax year in Greece, employer not resident in Greece, costs not borne by a Greek permanent establishment (Source 2).
4. Legally, a workation is usually not a "posting" within the meaning of the PWD, because no specific service is provided in Greece — the Greek PWD notification obligation (see existing article `entsendung-meldung-griechenland`) concerns the other, genuine posting case (Source 4).
5. Practical consequence for employers: apply for the A1 in advance (advisable even for short workations), keep a 183-day counter per tax year, check the registration requirement in Greece from day 91, PWD notification only where a genuine posting exists.

## Sources fetched and read (3, independent, official)

### 1. European Commission — Employment, Social Affairs & Inclusion, "Posted workers"
URL: https://employment-social-affairs.ec.europa.eu/policies-and-activities/moving-working-europe/working-another-eu-country/posted-workers_en
Fetched: 2026-08-30

Key facts/quotes:
- Definition: "A 'posted worker' is an employee who is sent by his employer to carry out a
  service in another EU Member State on a temporary basis, in the context of a contract of
  services, an intra-group posting or a hiring out through a temporary agency."
- Legal basis: Posting of Workers Directive 96/71/EC (1996), revised by Directive 2018/957/EU
  (2018), consolidated version 01996L0071-20200730.
- Core principle: posted workers remain employed by, and subject to the employment law of,
  the sending company/home Member State — but the PWD guarantees a set of mandatory host-country
  terms if more favourable: remuneration incl. overtime, max work/min rest periods, min paid
  annual leave, conditions of hiring-out via temp agencies, health/safety/hygiene at work,
  protective measures for pregnant workers/young people, equal treatment/non-discrimination,
  accommodation conditions (if employer-provided), travel/board/lodging expense reimbursement.
- Long-term posting rule: if posting exceeds 12 months (or 18 months with a motivated
  notification submitted), ALL host Member State terms and conditions of employment (by law or
  collective agreement, Art. 3(8)) apply — except conclusion/termination procedures of the
  employment contract (incl. non-compete clauses) and supplementary occupational retirement
  pension schemes.
- Enforcement Directive 2014/67/EU (2014) strengthens practical application: access to
  information, inspections/monitoring, joint liability in subcontracting chains, cross-border
  info exchange between Member States.
- European Labour Authority (ELA), established July 2019, is responsible for fair/simple/
  effective enforcement of labour mobility and social security coordination rules including
  posting.
- Social security of posted workers is governed separately by Regulation (EC) No 883/2004
  (coordination of social security systems) — this is the A1-certificate legal basis, distinct
  from the PWD's employment-law rules.
- Road transport sector has its own lex specialis: Directive 2020/1057 (Mobility Package I,
  applicable from 2 February 2022) for drivers.

### 2. European Labour Authority (ELA) — "Posting of workers" topic page
URL: https://www.ela.europa.eu/en/topics/posting-workers
Fetched: 2026-08-30

Key facts/quotes:
- Main sectors for posted workers: "construction, manufacturing, transport (road/freight),
  warehousing, financial, professional, scientific, and administrative activities, as well as
  human health and social work activities."
- Named implementation/enforcement challenges: accessibility/availability of information on
  applicable rules; complex mobility patterns (subcontracting schemes, letterbox companies,
  bogus self-employment); non-compliance cases; need for improved Member State cooperation.
- Growing phenomenon flagged by ELA: posting of third-country-national workers, who are
  "particularly vulnerable due to their dependence on employers for the renewal of work and
  residence permits" and at higher risk of abusive practices, labour-rights breaches, and
  irregular social-contribution payments.

### 3. German Customs (Zoll.de) — "Obligatory notification when workers are posted" (EN)
URL: https://www.zoll.de/EN/Businesses/Work/Foreign-domiciled-employers-posting/Obligatory-notification-workers-posted/obligatory-notification-workers-posted_node.html
Fetched: 2026-08-30

Key facts/quotes:
- "Foreign-domiciled employers posting workers to Germany to carry out work or to provide a
  service must comply with a number of rules for giving notification of their posted workers.
  The same applies to suppliers established abroad who hire employees to perform work or
  provide services in Germany."
- Notification route for Germany: online declaration via the "notification portal minimum wage"
  (Meldeportal Mindestlohn), legal basis Paragraph 1 MiLoMeldV — www.meldeportal-mindestlohn.de.
  Fax submissions no longer accepted.
- Road transport drivers posted to Germany from EU/EEA Member States: registration via the
  Internal Market Information System (IMI) / Road Transport Posting Declaration Portal
  (postingdeclaration.eu), not the minimum-wage portal. UK-established road transport
  undertakings may alternatively use IMI. Third-country road transport undertakings still use
  the minimum-wage reporting portal.
- Confirms Germany implements the PWD via national minimum-wage/posting-notification law
  (MiLoG/MiLoMeldV), separate from the EU-level PWD/Enforcement Directive framework and separate
  from the A1-certificate social-security process (which runs through DRV/health insurers/
  SV-Meldeportal, not Zoll).

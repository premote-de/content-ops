## Source 1: Deutsche Rentenversicherung (German pension insurance, DRV) — A1 certificate (A1-Bescheinigung) FAQ
URL: https://www.deutsche-rentenversicherung.de/SharedDocs/FAQ/a1_bescheinigung/a1_bescheinigung_faq_liste
Retrieved: 2026-08-25

Quotes/facts (translated from the German original):
- "If these persons work only temporarily in another Member State of the European Union (EU), in Iceland, Liechtenstein, Norway, Switzerland or the United Kingdom [...] (so-called posting), the law of the sending state exceptionally continues to apply."
- Personal scope: A1 for a posting (Entsendung) to Switzerland only possible for nationals of the EU/Switzerland — example: Norwegian or Turkish nationals can NOT obtain a German A1 certificate for a posting to Switzerland ("personal scope not met").
- "From 1 January 2025, the A1 certificate must always be applied for electronically. Applying [...] with a paper form is not permitted."
- Short business trips of up to 7 days: A1 can be applied for retroactively (with reference to CJEU case law / BMAS (Federal Ministry of Labour and Social Affairs) guidance).
- "If the business trip is to states in which intensified checks are carried out (in particular France, Austria and Switzerland), it is advisable to apply for the A1 certificate in advance where appropriate."
- Competence: health insurance fund (statutorily insured), ABV (privately insured + covered by a professional pension scheme), pension insurance institution (privately insured, not covered by a professional scheme), GKV-Spitzenverband/DVKA (German liaison office for health insurance abroad; multi-state activity, habitually in more than one Member State — "at least one day per month or five days per quarter").
- Multi-state activity: A1 possible for up to 5 years for all Member States concerned.
- Cross-border telework: A1 required as soon as the home-office share in the state of residence reaches ≥25%; exception agreement (Ausnahmevereinbarung) possible at 25–<50% (application to the DVKA).

## Source 2: EAK Switzerland (Eidgenössische Ausgleichskasse, Swiss Federal Compensation Office) — A1 certificate, Certificate of Coverage (CoC)
URL: https://www.eak.admin.ch/de/bescheinigung-a1-certificate-of-coverage-coc
Retrieved: 2026-08-25

Quotes/facts (translated from the German original):
- "All certificates should be applied for in good time and preferably via the ALPS portal (https://www.alps.bsv.admin.ch/alps) provided by the Federal Social Insurance Office."
- "With a posting certificate (A1, CoC), the employee can prove to foreign authorities that Swiss social security law applies to them during their assignment abroad."
- "Well-known examples [of states with sanctions for a missing certificate] are France and Austria."
- Exception: "for very short, one-off assignments abroad (business trips, seminars, participation in exhibitions and trade fairs), applying for the posting certificate [can] be dispensed with for reasons of proportionality. If needed, the certificate can also be applied for retroactively."
- For multi-state activities (EU/EFTA), an A1 must always be applied for.

## Source 3: Compensation office (Ausgleichskasse) Basel-Stadt (ak-bs.ch) — A1 certificate (employers)
URL: https://ak-bs.ch/internationales/arbeitgebende/a1-bescheinigung/
Retrieved: 2026-08-25

Quotes/facts (translated from the German original):
- "A1 is the designation of an EU form which confirms the applicable social security legislation for activities in the EU or EFTA."
- "Third-country nationals - from outside Switzerland, the EU or EFTA – do not fall within the scope of form A1 in the context of relations between Switzerland and the EU, Iceland, Liechtenstein and Norway." (confirms the DRV point on personal scope from the Swiss perspective)
- "This form certifies the application of Swiss legislation for postings of up to 24 months and for simultaneous activities in several states."
- Competent body in Switzerland: the respective compensation office (organised at cantonal level, e.g. ak-bs.ch, svazurich.ch).
- Non-working accompanying spouses can join the AHV (Swiss old-age and survivors' insurance) voluntarily (deadline: 6 months).

## Implications for the article
- Core difference from the existing generic A1 article (a1-bescheinigung-basics): Switzerland is bound to Regulation (EC) No 883/2004 via the bilateral Switzerland–EU Agreement on the Free Movement of Persons (Freizügigkeitsabkommen) and formally uses the same A1 form — but with its own authority structure (compensation offices instead of health insurance funds/DRV for postings FROM Switzerland; EAK/BSV ALPS portal), its own portal (ALPS instead of the SV-Meldeportal) and the nationality restriction (only EU/EFTA/CH nationals, see the DRV Norway/Turkey example above).
- Two perspectives needed: (1) German company posts employees to Switzerland → A1 via German health insurance fund/DRV/DVKA, inspection risk in CH explicitly named by the DRV. (2) Swiss company posts employees to the EU → A1/CoC via compensation office, ALPS portal.
- Existing article "arbeitsbewilligung-schweiz" (PR #7) covers the 90-day limit for third-country nationals/work permit — NOT the A1/social security side. No content overlap, but linking makes sense (both Switzerland compliance topics).

## Source 1 — Royal Thai Consulate-General Munich (official)
URL: https://munich.thaiembassy.org/de/page/visaexemption (retrieved 2026-08-26)
Quotes (translated from the German original text of the page): "Citizens of the countries listed above may enter Thailand without a visa for purely tourist purposes and stay in Thailand for up to 60 days per entry." Further: "Please note that visa exemption entry into the Kingdom of Thailand is at the sole discretion of the immigration officer. Entry into Thailand by air may take place any number of times. Entry by land and sea is limited to a maximum of 2 entries per calendar year."
Addition on work during visa exemption: "If you enter the Kingdom of Thailand with a visa exemption and need to perform necessary or urgent work or ad hoc work, please ask the Thai Immigration Bureau or the labour office in Thailand for permission... This necessary/urgent/ad hoc work should be completed within 15 working days."
→ Facts: 60 days visa-free per entry for German citizens (tourism); discretionary decision of the immigration officer; entry by land/sea max. 2x per calendar year; regular work during visa exemption not permitted as a rule, only narrowly defined exceptions with official permission.

## Source 2 — Royal Thai Consulate-General Los Angeles, DTV page (official)
URL: https://thaiconsulatela.thaiembassy.org/en/publicservice/dtv-visa (retrieved 2026-08-26, last updated according to the page 3 Dec 2025)
Quote: "Destination Thailand Visa (DTV)... Purpose of Visit: Workcation (Digital nomad/remote worker/foreign talent/freelancer)... Thai soft power related activities e.g. Muaythai, Thai culinary training and medical treatment... Spouse and children under 20 years old of DTV visa holders."
Required documents (workcation category): passport (validity ≥6 months), current photo, proof of current place of residence, bank statement with a balance of ≥500,000 THB (≈17,000 USD), employment contract/employment certificate or freelancer portfolio as proof of digital nomad/remote worker status.
Note on extension: "For an extension of stay, please contact the Immigration Bureau directly once you are in Thailand."
→ Facts: the DTV is explicitly aimed at digital nomads/remote workers/freelancers; proof of funds 500,000 THB; extension is handled by the Immigration Bureau in Thailand, not by the embassy.

## Source 3 — BDO Thailand (tax advisory, specialist source)
URL: https://www.bdo.th/en-gb/insights/destination-thailand-visa-(dtv) (retrieved 2026-08-26, article date 30 Jul 2024)
Quote: "The DTV is a multiple entry visa that allows its holder to stay in Thailand for up to 180 days per visit, with option to extend for an additional 180 days each time. The DTV is valid for 5 years... The DTV Announcement was published in the government's official Gazette on 15 July 2024 and takes effect immediately."
Fee: "The DTV fee ranges from Baht 10,000 to 50,000 THB depending on the country of application."
Tax note: "If the DTV holder stays in Thailand for less than 180 days per year, his foreign source income remitted to Thailand will not be taxable in Thailand. Conversely, if the DTV holder extends his/her stay in Thailand for another 180 days, his/her foreign source remitted income will be taxable."
Further note: "The DTV holder must not engage in Thai related businesses of his offshore employer... If the DTV holder would like to work for a Thai employer, a work permit is required."
→ Facts: DTV valid 5 years, multiple entry, 180 days per stay + one-time extension by a further 180 days, fee 10,000–50,000 THB depending on the country of application, legal basis: Gazette publication 15.07.2024, tax consequence from 180 days/year of stay.

## Key messages for the article, in context
1. Visa-free (visa exemption): 60 days per entry for German citizens, at the discretion of the immigration officer (Source 1).
2. DTV (Destination Thailand Visa): valid 5 years, multiple entry, 180 days each + one-time extension by 180 days, intended for digital nomads/remote workers/freelancers, proof of funds 500,000 THB, fee 10,000–50,000 THB (Sources 2+3).
3. Tax threshold: From a cumulative 180 days of stay in Thailand per calendar year, foreign income transferred to Thailand potentially becomes taxable under Thai tax law (Source 3) — link to premote's core compliance topic (permanent establishment (Betriebsstätte)/residence risk in workations), linking to the existing articles 183-tage-regel, betriebsstaettenrisiko, digital-nomad-visa.
4. Working during a pure visa exemption (without DTV) is not permitted as a rule — only narrow exceptions for "necessary/urgent/ad-hoc work" with official permission (Source 1). Important clarification for workation travellers who choose Thailand as a destination.

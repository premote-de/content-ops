## Sources (all retrieved and read in this session)

### 1. MDEC — DE Rantau (official Malaysia Digital Economy Corporation, programme operator)
URL: https://mdec.my/derantau (live version is JS-rendered, therefore read via Wayback snapshot of 2026-08-04: https://web.archive.org/web/20260804142342/https://www.mdec.my/derantau — snapshot date confirmed, content is the programme page currently linked by MDEC)
Key statements (verbatim/paraphrased):
- "DE Rantau is Malaysia's flagship initiative to establish the country as the premier digital nomad hub in Southeast Asia."
- Pass type: **Professional Visit Pass (Pas Lawatan Ikhtisas / PLIK)**.
- Duration: **3 to 12 months**, extendable by up to a further 12 months.
- Fee: **MYR 1,000 (main applicant), MYR 500 (dependants)**.
- Spouse/children may accompany; parents only in the case of the main applicant.
- Two application categories: **Tech Talent/Profession** and **Non-Tech Talent/Profession**.
  - Tech: Digital Freelancer, Independent Contractor, Remote Worker in IT/digital roles (Software Engineer, Cloud, Cybersecurity, Blockchain, AI/ML, Digital Marketing, UX/UI etc.)
  - Non-Tech (admitted since June 2024, see Source 2): CEO, COO, CFO, Business Development, Marketing Manager, Finance/Accountant, Sales Manager, HR Manager, Legal Counsel, PR Manager, Consultant, Customer Success, Technical Writer, Tax Specialist/Accountant etc.
- Proof of activity required:
  - Freelancer/Independent Contractor: active project contract, term > 3 months (several contracts also possible), clients local or foreign.
  - Remote Worker: active employment contract, term > 3 months, employer must be foreign/non-Malaysian.
- Minimum income:
  - Tech Talent: **> USD 24,000/year**
  - Non-Tech Talent: **> USD 60,000/year**
- Full FAQ (primary source, PDF): https://www.mdec.my/static/pdf/derantau/251105_DE%20Rantau_Pass_FAQ_V8.pdf (file name carries the date 2025-11-05 in the PDF name — latest FAQ version; PDF binary content not text-extractable in this session, but the key points are duplicated on the MDEC programme page itself and were read from there).

### 2. Ministry of Digital Malaysia — official press release on the expansion of the eligibility criteria
URL: https://www.digital.gov.my/en-GB/siaran/DE-Rantau-Nomad-Pass-eligibility-expanded (retrieved directly, HTML, read 2026-08-31)
Key statements:
- Announcement by Minister of Digital YB Gobind Singh Deo (DE Rantau Elevate 2024, KL Sentral): eligibility expanded from purely IT/digital roles to further occupational groups (Founder, CEO, COO, tax advisor, legal advisor, Technical Writer, Business Development Manager, PR etc.).
- "The minimum income requirement for non-IT and non-digital talents is set at USD60,000 per year, or USD5,000 per month." — independently confirms the value from Source 1.
- After the 2-year DE Rantau period expires, two follow-on options: Malaysian Tech Entrepreneur Programme (Residence Pass, 5 years) or Malaysia Digital Status with company formation → Foreign Knowledge Worker Employment Pass.
- Programme launch year: **2022**.
- Malaysia according to the Remote ranking "Best Destinations for Remote Work 2023": rank 22 (up from rank 84 the previous year); according to nomadlist.com, the most-liked country among digital nomads in December 2023.

### 3. PwC Malaysia — Malaysia Tax Booklet, Personal Income Tax (independent audit firm, summarises LHDN/Income Tax Act)
URL: https://www.pwc.com/my/en/publications/mtb/personal-income-tax.html (retrieved directly, HTML, read 2026-08-31)
Key statements:
- **182-day rule** (tax residency): a person is tax resident in Malaysia if physically present for at least 182 days in the calendar year. Alternative: a shorter presence that is linked to a period of ≥182 consecutive days in the preceding or following year (short interruptions for business trips, medical treatment or social visits ≤14 days continue to count as continuous presence).
- Tax rates for residents (tax resident) YA 2025/2026: progressive from 0% (up to MYR 5,000) to 30% (from MYR 2.000.000 chargeable income), base table with brackets at MYR 20,000/35.000/50.000/70.000/100.000/400.000/600.000.
- Tax rates for non-residents (not tax resident): business/employment income at a flat **30%** (no allowance, no progression) — relevant for DE Rantau holders who stay under 182 days but still have Malaysia-sourced taxable income.
- Reference: this is the substantive basis for Public Ruling No. 6/2011 issued by LHDN (Inland Revenue Board Malaysia) on the 182-day rule under Section 7(1) Income Tax Act 1967 (LHDN original documents at hasil.gov.my/phl.hasil.gov.my are PDF binaries that were technically not text-extractable in this session — PwC, as an audit firm, summarises the same statutory text and is used here as a secondary source for the figures, while the MDEC/Ministry sources deliver the programme details directly from the operator).

## Applied premote perspective (implications for the article)
Target audience: German employers/HR whose employees are planning a workation in Malaysia (not: the Malaysian authorities' perspective on immigrants in general).
Relevant compliance points for the article:
1. The DE Rantau Pass is NO substitute for social security obligations in Germany — Malaysia is not an EU/EEA/CH state and has no social security agreement (Sozialversicherungsabkommen) with Germany for A1 purposes (unlike a1-bescheinigung-usa: there the D/USA agreement applies). For Malaysia, German social security remains relevant under the general Ausstrahlung principles (section 4 of the Social Code Book IV, § 4 SGB IV — continued application of German social security law during temporary work abroad), provided the posting (Entsendung)/workation is time-limited and a German employment contract continues — this must be assessed independently of the DE Rantau Pass.
2. Permanent establishment (Betriebsstätte) tax risk: the Non-Tech remote worker category requires an "active employment contract with a foreign (non-Malaysian) employer" — this matches the classic premote workation case (German employer, employee works temporarily from Malaysia).
3. The 182-day threshold is the central risk line for Malaysian tax residency — workations should stay well below it to avoid Malaysian wage tax liability; if exceeded, Malaysian tax liability threatens in addition to the German one (double taxation risk; no double taxation agreement (DBA) reference for Malaysia-Germany verified in the sources checked — flag as a check point in the article, do not present as a confirmed statement unless the DBA can be verified).
4. The pass itself only governs residence status (immigration), not any exemption from social security or wage tax — this difference must be made explicit in the article (typical misconception: "visa = automatically compliant").

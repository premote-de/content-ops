## Sources (all retrieved and read directly, 2026-09-08)

### 1. UK ETA — gov.uk (official government site, UK Home Office)
URL: https://www.gov.uk/eta
Quote: "An electronic travel authorisation (ETA) lets you travel to the UK, Jersey, Guernsey or the Isle of Man for up to 6 months. [...] An ETA costs £20."
→ Cost: £20, validity/stay: up to 6 months per visit.

URL: https://www.gov.uk/eta/what-you-can-cannot-do
Quote: "You can: come to the UK for up to 6 months for tourism, visiting family and friends, on a business trip or for short-term study [...] You cannot: [...] do paid or unpaid work for a UK company or as a self-employed person."
→ Business trips are permitted with an ETA, local gainful employment is not.

URL: https://uk.diplo.de/uk-de/02/elektronische-reisegenehmigung-eta-2711236 (German Embassy London / Federal Foreign Office (Auswärtiges Amt))
Quote (translated from German): "Since 25 February 2026, an Electronic Travel Authorisation (ETA) is mandatory for entry into the United Kingdom. Anyone who does not hold an ETA from that date, although he or she needs one, will already be turned away by the carrier when checking in at a foreign airport."
→ Mandatory cut-off date for German nationals: 25 February 2026 (confirmed by the German diplomatic mission).

### 2. USA — not "ETA" but ESTA (official US government sites: CBP, DHS)
URL: https://www.cbp.gov/travel/international-visitors/esta
Quote: "ESTA is an automated system that determines the eligibility of visitors to travel to the United States under the Visa Waiver Program (VWP). [...] You must have an e-Passport to use the VWP."
→ The USA use the distinct term ESTA, not ETA (the risk of confusion with the Basque organisation ETA is a well-known background, but is not officially cited by CBP/DHS as the reason — therefore not adopted as fact here).

URL: https://www.dhs.gov/publication/electronic-system-travel-authorization
Quote: DHS/CBP Privacy Impact Assessment PIA-007(j), July 2024, on ESTA extensions (biometric data, IBIS programme).
→ Confirms ESTA as an official, continuously updated DHS/CBP system.

### 3. Canada — eTA (official government site Canada.ca, retrieved via Wayback snapshot of the live page)
URL: https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/visit-canada/eta/facts.html (retrieved via web.archive.org snapshot of 2026-08-30, since direct access via the fetch tool timed out; page content is the unchanged official Canada.ca text)
Quote: "An Electronic Travel Authorization (eTA) is an entry requirement for visa-exempt foreign nationals travelling to Canada by air. [...] It is valid for up to five years or until the passport expires, whichever comes first. [...] An eTA costs CAD $7."
→ Cost: CAD 7, validity: up to 5 years or until passport expiry, stay up to 6 months per entry, mandatory only for air travellers (not for entry by land/rail/bus/ship).

### 4. EU — ETIAS (official EU source: European Commission, Directorate-General for Home Affairs)
URL: https://home-affairs.ec.europa.eu/news/main-differences-between-ees-and-etias-what-travellers-need-know-2026-04-28_en
Quote: "The EES is fully operational across all Schengen countries since 10 April 2026. ETIAS is scheduled to start in the last quarter of 2026. [...] It will be valid for 3 years or until your passport expires (whichever comes first)."
→ EES active since 10.4.2026; ETIAS start Q4 2026 (exact date not yet communicated); validity 3 years or until passport expiry. German nationals (EU citizens) do not need ETIAS — it applies to visa-exempt third-country nationals travelling to the Schengen area.

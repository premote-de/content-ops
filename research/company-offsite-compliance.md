## Sources (all retrieved and read first-hand)

### 1. Deutsche Rentenversicherung (German pension insurance, DRV) — A1 certificate FAQ (official institution page)
URL: https://www.deutsche-rentenversicherung.de/SharedDocs/FAQ/a1_bescheinigung/a1_bescheinigung_faq_liste
Retrieved: 2026-08-29

Key messages (quotes translated from the German original):
- An A1 certificate (A1-Bescheinigung) is needed as soon as someone is temporarily gainfully employed in another EU/EEA/CH/UK country ("posting") — the legal text makes no distinction between a classic posting (Entsendung) and one-off activities such as conferences, workshops or team events. Quote: "If these persons work only temporarily in another Member State of the European Union (EU) [...] (so-called posting), the law of the sending state exceptionally continues to apply."
- Exception for short-term/brief business trips of up to 7 days: the A1 can be applied for retroactively (CJEU case law, BMAS (Federal Ministry of Labour and Social Affairs) guidance). Quote: "For short-term or brief business trips of up to seven days, the A1 certificate can, if required, be applied for retroactively."
- Since 1 January 2025, applications may only be submitted electronically (paper applications not permitted), via payroll software or the SV-Meldeportal (social insurance notification portal).
- Increased inspections especially in France, Austria and Switzerland — here, applying in advance rather than retroactively is recommended.
- If the A1 is missing during an inspection, fines and on-site delays are possible (context from the FAQ text on tightened inspections to combat undeclared work/wage dumping).

Relevance for the article: a multi-day company offsite in another EU country with German participants in principle triggers the A1 obligation like any other cross-border gainful activity — the 7-day exception covers short kick-offs; multi-day offsites (typically 2-5 days) often sit right at the threshold.

### 2. German Social Accident Insurance (DGUV) — insurance abroad / statutory accident insurance abroad (official umbrella association of the statutory accident insurance institutions)
URL: https://www.dguv.de/de/internationales/sozialrecht/auslandsversicherung/index.jsp
Retrieved: 2026-08-29

Key messages (quote translated from the German original):
- German statutory accident insurance cover in principle continues to apply during a posting/temporary work abroad ("Ausstrahlung", i.e. the extraterritorial application of German social security law), provided the requirements are met. Quote: "If the employee leaves the German social security system because of a stay abroad that is initially of unlimited duration or because the requirements attached to a posting are not met, some accident insurance institutions offer voluntary insurance abroad (AUV) [...] (Social Code Book VII (SGB VII) sections 140 ff.)."
- For cases in which regular cover no longer applies (e.g. unclear duration, no classic posting relationship), several Berufsgenossenschaften (statutory accident insurance institutions: BGRCI, BGHM, BG BAU, BG ETEM, BGHW, VBG, BGW, UVB, BGN) offer voluntary insurance abroad.
- European rules or social security agreements (Sozialversicherungsabkommen) must not conflict with the voluntary insurance abroad — i.e. within the EU/EEA/CH the A1 logic takes precedence; the voluntary AUV is more relevant for third countries without an agreement.

Relevance for the article: for offsites abroad, companies must ensure that participants are covered by statutory insurance in the event of an accident (e.g. during team-building activities, excursions, transfers) — cover in principle "travels along" via the A1 logic, but special cases (third countries, unclear duration) require additional review/voluntary insurance abroad.

### 3. JUHN Partner — permanent establishment (Betriebsstätte) under Fiscal Code (AO) section 12 and OECD Model Tax Convention (OECD-MA) Art. 5 (independent tax law firm, academic paper with FOM University)
URL: https://www.juhn.com/fachwissen/internationales-steuerrecht/betriebsstaette-12-ao-art-5-oecd-ma/
Retrieved: 2026-08-29

Key messages (quote translated from the German original):
- A permanent establishment under Art. 5(1) OECD-MA requires a fixed place of business at the disposal of the enterprise, set up for a certain duration, through which the business is carried on — mere temporary presence (e.g. a multi-day offsite in a rented conference room/hotel) regularly does NOT meet the permanence criterion of a fixed place of business.
- Negative list in Art. 5(4) OECD-MA: facilities used solely for preparatory or auxiliary activities do not constitute a permanent establishment, even if they meet the other criteria. Quote: "These are, for example, facilities [...] that are [intended] solely to carry out work not belonging to the enterprise's business, preparatory activities and auxiliary activities."
- Delimitation matters: if a core activity is carried out at the facility (e.g. contract negotiations, operational management, customer business), the exception may no longer apply — the classification depends on the individual case based on the activity carried out, not on duration alone.

Relevance for the article: a pure team offsite (strategy workshop, team building, kick-off without customer business) as a rule does not constitute a permanent establishment — already for lack of permanence AND because it is an internal, preparatory/organisational activity. The risk increases if actual core operating business takes place during the offsite (contract signings, customer presentations with negotiating authority) or the "offsite location" de facto becomes a recurring field office.

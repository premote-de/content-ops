## Facts + sources (all retrieved and read first-hand, 2026-08-27)

### 1. Deutsche Rentenversicherung (German pension insurance, DRV) — official FAQ (DE, reference source for the legal basis)
URL: https://www.deutsche-rentenversicherung.de/SharedDocs/FAQ/a1_bescheinigung/a1_bescheinigung_faq_liste
Retrieved: 2026-08-27
Quote (translated from German): "If these persons work only temporarily in another Member State of the European Union (EU), in Iceland, Liechtenstein, Norway, Switzerland or the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Northern Ireland (so-called posting), the law of the sending state exceptionally continues to apply. With an A1 certificate, the working persons can prove whether the law of the sending state or the provisions of a foreign state are applicable to them."
→ Country scope confirmed: EU + Iceland, Liechtenstein, Norway, Switzerland, UK.

### 2. European Labour Authority (ELA) — official EU glossary (EN)
URL: https://www.ela.europa.eu/en/glossary/a1-form
Retrieved: 2026-08-27
Quote (full definition): "Portable document certifying that a mobile worker in the European Union or EFTA countries and Switzerland is registered in the social security system of the country that issued it."
→ Official EN short definition, directly quotable.

### 3. European Commission — EU social security coordination (EN)
URL: https://employment-social-affairs.ec.europa.eu/policies-and-activities/moving-working-europe/eu-social-security-coordination_en
Retrieved: 2026-08-27
Quote: "The rules apply across the EU, as well as Iceland, Liechtenstein, Norway and Switzerland. Specific coordination rules also apply to the United Kingdom." And: "The EU rules on social security coordination, set out in Regulations 883/2004 and 987/2009, have applied since 2010. In April 2026, EU countries approved revised rules that better reflect changes in labour markets and national social security systems... Workers must be insured in their home country for at least three months before being posted abroad. After 24 months of posting, there must be a break of at least two months before another posting."
→ IMPORTANT: confirms the legal basis (Regulation 883/2004 + 987/2009, since 2010) AND a rule reform in April 2026 (3-month prior insurance, 2-month break after 24 months of posting). Include this reform information in the article, marked with date and source, since it is new and not yet widely known.

### 4. EUR-Lex — Recommendation No A1 (Administrative Commission, 2018/C 183/06)
URL: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32018H0529(01)
Retrieved: 2026-08-27
Quote: "Article 19(2) of Regulation (EC) No 987/2009 provides that at the request of the person concerned or of the employer, the competent institution of the Member State whose legislation is applicable pursuant to Title II of Regulation (EC) No 883/2004 shall provide an attestation that such legislation is applicable... The Administrative Commission determines the structure and the content of the Portable Document A1 concerning the applicable legislation which applies to the holder."
→ Legal basis for the form/structure of the A1 (Portable Document A1), legal basis Art. 19(2) Regulation 987/2009 + Art. 72(a) Regulation 883/2004.

### 5. UK HMRC — National Insurance Manual NIM33115 (gov.uk, official, EN)
URL: https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/national-insurance-manual/nim33115
Retrieved: 2026-08-27
Quote: "A Portable Document A1 must be produced by a person for an agreement to be made for them to be exempt from United Kingdom National Insurance; otherwise Class 1 National Insurance will be charged." And: "In the United Kingdom portable documents (A1) are issued by:- HM Revenue and Customs, Personal Tax Directorate, National Insurance Contributions & Employer Office, International Caseworker Room BP1301, Benton Park View, Newcastle Upon Tyne NE98 1ZZ"
→ Concrete evidence of the consequence of having no A1 in the UK context (Class 1 NI becomes due) — supports the "what happens without an A1" section for the UK target group.

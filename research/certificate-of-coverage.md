## Source 1 — SSA.gov (official, USA), via Wayback snapshot 2026-08-24
URL: https://web.archive.org/web/20260824033503/https://www.ssa.gov/international/agreements_overview.html
(Live ssa.gov blocks automated fetches with 403/Access Denied — the snapshot is identical official content, page "U.S. International Social Security Agreements".)

Quotes:
- "International Social Security agreements, often called 'Totalization agreements,' have two main purposes. First, they eliminate dual Social Security taxation, the situation that occurs when a worker from one country works in another country and is required to pay Social Security taxes to both countries on the same earnings. Second, the agreements help fill gaps in benefit protection for workers who have divided their careers between the United States and another country."
- List of US agreement countries with date of entry into force (excerpt): Germany – December 1, 1979; United Kingdom – January 1, 1985; Canada – August 1, 1984; Australia – October 1, 2002; Switzerland – November 1, 1980. (A total of 30 countries in the list, from Italy 1978 to Iceland 2019.)
- "Workers who are exempt from U.S. or foreign Social Security taxes under an agreement must document their exemption by obtaining a certificate of coverage from the country that will continue to cover them. For example, a U.S. worker sent on temporary assignment to the United Kingdom would need a certificate of coverage issued by SSA to prove his or her exemption from U.K. Social Security contributions. Conversely, a U.K.-based employee working temporarily in the United States would need a certificate from the U.K. authorities as evidence of the exemption from U.S. Social Security tax."
- Germany entry (from Totalization Contacts, snapshot 2026-05-21, https://web.archive.org/web/20260521021242/https://www.ssa.gov/international/totalization_contacts.html): "To establish your exemption from coverage under the U.S. Social Security system, your employer in Germany must request a certificate of coverage (form D/USA 101) from the local German Sickness Fund that collects your social security taxes in Germany."
- Detached-worker rule: "a person who is temporarily transferred to work for the same employer in another country remains covered only by the country from which he or she has been sent […] The detached-worker rule in U.S. agreements generally applies to employees whose assignments in the host country are expected to last 5 years or less."

## Source 2 — DVKA (German liaison office for health insurance abroad; GKV-Spitzenverband, official, Germany), live fetched 2026-09-11
URL: https://www.dvka.de/de/arbeitgeber-erwerbstaetige/laenderspezifische-informationen/abkommenstaaten/usa/entsendung.html
(Last updated according to the page: 26.05.2026)

Quotes (translated from the German original):
- "A posting within the meaning of the agreement exists when a person habitually employed in Germany is sent by their employer to the USA for a period limited in advance, within the framework of an employment relationship existing in Germany […] For them, the German social security legislation continues to apply for the maximum five-year duration of the posting to the USA. If the assignment is limited in advance to more than 5 years, a posting within the meaning of the agreement is not possible."
- "As proof of the continued application of German social security legislation, certificate D/USA 101 can, in the case of a posting, be applied for in Germany at the following bodies: the statutory health insurance fund to which the contributions to statutory pension insurance are paid (collection agency), or the GKV-Spitzenverband, DVKA, if no pension insurance contributions are payable to a statutory health insurance fund in Germany."
- "Applications for the issue of a certificate D/USA 101 for employed persons must be submitted exclusively electronically to one of the aforementioned bodies."
- "If a second assignment to the USA takes place, a new posting is only assumed if at least twelve months have passed since the previous work assignment or if the assignment as a whole is not expected to exceed five years."
- "If the expected duration of the posting exceeds the period of five years, American legislation applies to the employed person immediately as a rule […] However, the continued application of German legislation is possible by concluding an exception agreement (Ausnahmevereinbarung)."

## Implications for the article
- "Certificate of Coverage" is an umbrella term for the proof of continued social security coverage under bilateral totalization/social security agreements (Sozialversicherungsabkommen) (outside the EU/EEA/Switzerland coordination, which uses the A1 certificate (A1-Bescheinigung) instead).
- For Germany-USA specifically: form D/USA 101, issued by the competent health insurance fund (collection agency) or the DVKA, max. 5 years standard posting duration, exception agreement possible for longer assignments, exclusively electronic procedure for some time now.
- For the UK: form CA9107 at HMRC, for a defined list of non-EU/EEA countries (incl. USA, Canada, Japan, India and others) — for EU/EEA/Switzerland a different (A1-equivalent) procedure applies instead.
- For other countries (Canada, Australia, Switzerland) similarly their own forms (CPT56A/QUE-USA101 for Canada according to the SSA contacts page; Certificate of Coverage AUS/USA1 for Australia according to the SSA contacts page) — already covered in the premote wiki via the country-specific digital nomad visa articles (Canada, Australia) and a1-bescheinigung-usa/-schweiz; the new article bundles the umbrella term and links to them.

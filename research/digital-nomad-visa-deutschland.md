# Research: Digital Nomad Visa Germany (Cluster)

Keywords: digital nomad visa germany / germany digital nomad visa / does germany have a digital nomad visa / german digital nomad visa (+ DE counterparts: freiberufler-visum (freelancer visa), digital nomad visum deutschland)
Cluster key message: Germany has NO dedicated "Digital Nomad Visa" — the real routes are section 21 of the Residence Act (AufenthG) (freelancers/self-employed), the EU Blue Card, and the 90/180-day Schengen limit for remote work on a short stay.

## Source 1 — Residence Act (Aufenthaltsgesetz) section 21 (self-employed activity)
URL: https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/aufenthg_2004/__21.html (live fetch timeout; retrieved and read via Wayback snapshot 2026-09-02: http://web.archive.org/web/2026/https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/aufenthg_2004/__21.html)
Official statutory text, retrieved 2026-09-06. Quotes (translated from the German original):

Verbatim quote, subsection 1: "A foreigner may be granted a residence permit for the purpose of pursuing a self-employed activity if 1. there is an economic interest or a regional need, 2. the activity can be expected to have positive effects on the economy, and 3. the financing of its implementation is secured through equity capital or a loan commitment."

Subsection 4: "The residence permit is limited to a maximum of three years. After three years, a settlement permit may be granted, notwithstanding section 9(2), if the foreigner has been self-employed for three years..."

Subsection 5 (freelancers, the paragraph central to the article): "A foreigner may be granted a residence permit for the purpose of pursuing a freelance activity, notwithstanding subsection 1. Any licence required to practise the liberal profession must have been granted or its granting must have been promised. Subsection 1 sentence 3 applies accordingly. Subsection 4 does not apply." — Consequence: for freelancers, the 3-year time-limit rule from subsection 4 is explicitly dropped (no automatic route to a settlement permit after 3 years as for business operators under subsection 1).

Subsection 3: "Foreigners who are older than 45 years should only be granted the residence permit if they have adequate provision for old age."

## Source 3 — Make it in Germany, "EU Blue Card"
URL: https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/en/visa-residence/types/eu-blue-card (live bot block; retrieved and read via Wayback snapshot: http://web.archive.org/web/2026/https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/en/visa-residence/types/eu-blue-card)
Official BMWK portal, as of 2026, retrieved 2026-09-06.

Quote (salary thresholds 2026): "Your job in Germany must enable you to earn a gross annual salary of at least €50,700 (as of 2026). If you are employed in a shortage occupation, you can also obtain an EU Blue Card with a lower gross annual salary of at least €45,934.20 (as of 2026) if the Federal Employment Agency (BA) has approved your employment." Further condition: employment duration of at least 6 months, the job must match the academic qualification.

Quote on young professionals: "If you obtained your last degree or equivalent qualification less than three years ago, you can also obtain an EU Blue Card if your job offer includes a gross annual salary of at least €45,934.20 (as of 2026)."

Relevance for the article: the Blue Card is NOT intended for freelancers/remote workers with a foreign employer — it requires a concrete employment contract with a company in Germany. Important distinction in the article.

## Source 4 — European Commission, "Your Europe" citizens' portal (90/180-day rule)
URL: https://europa.eu/youreurope/citizens/travel/entry-exit/non-eu-nationals/index_en.htm
Official EU citizens' portal, retrieved live and read 2026-09-06.

Quote: "The EU has a common set of visa rules which apply to short stays (that is up to 90 days in any 180-day period) and for transit through international transit areas of airports in the Schengen area. These rules apply in 29 European countries: 25 EU countries [...] Germany [...] as well as 4 non-EU countries: Iceland, Liechtenstein, Norway and Switzerland."

Relevance: travellers who continue to work remotely for a foreign employer on a visa-free short stay (tourist status) are in a legal grey area — the 90/180-day limit caps this at 90 days at most anyway, and in any case it is NOT a residence title for gainful employment in Germany.

## Summary for the article
1. Germany has no "Digital Nomad Visa" as a visa category of its own.
2. Freelancers/self-employed: section 21(5) AufenthG (freelancer residence permit), no fixed 3-year limit as for business operators under subsection 1 (subsection 4 explicitly excluded), but in practice likewise usually limited to several years and extendable; proof of old-age provision from age 45.
3. Employees with an employment contract with a company in Germany and a university degree: EU Blue Card, salary threshold 2026 €50,700 (shortage occupations: €45,934,20).
4. Pure remote work for a foreign employer without a German residence title: only possible as a short stay within the 90/180-day Schengen rule, no work authorisation, no substitute for a right of residence — for workation-type stays, the A1 certificate (A1-Bescheinigung)/social security law must additionally be checked separately (see a1-certificate-explained, workation-basics).

## Source 1 — OECD Model Tax Convention (OECD-MA), Art. 4(2) (official wording)
URL: https://legalinstruments.oecd.org/public/doc/471/12f5c630-3029-41a7-9f20-84b8cd2d822a.htm
Retrieved: 2026-09-05

Quote (Art. 4(2), tie-breaker cascade):
> "2. Where by reason of the provisions of paragraph 1 an individual is a resident of both Contracting States, then his status shall be determined as follows: (a) he shall be deemed to be a resident of the State in which he has a permanent home available to him; if he has a permanent home available to him in both States, he shall be deemed to be a resident of the State with which his personal and economic relations are closer (centre of vital interests); (b) if the State in which he has his centre of vital interests cannot be determined, or if he has not a permanent home available to him in either State, he shall be deemed to be a resident of the State in which he has an habitual abode; (c) if he has an habitual abode in both States or in neither of them, he shall be deemed to be a resident of the State of which he is a national; (d) if he is a national of both States or of neither of them, the competent authorities of the Contracting States shall settle the question by mutual agreement."

This yields the five-step order of examination: 1. permanent home (ständige Wohnstätte) → 2. centre of vital interests (Mittelpunkt der Lebensinteressen) → 3. habitual abode (gewöhnlicher Aufenthalt) → 4. nationality → 5. mutual agreement procedure.

Key paragraphs (paras. 12-24, "Bestimmung der Ansässigkeit - Art. 4 OECD-MA" (determination of residence – Art. 4 OECD-MA); quotes translated from the German original):

Para. 12: "For the application of the DBA, a person can be considered resident in only one contracting state. If the person is resident in both contracting states under Art. 4(1) sentence 1 OECD-MA (so-called dual residence), the contracting state in which the person is deemed to be resident must be determined according to the order of examination laid down in Art. 4(2) OECD-MA."

Para. 13 (definition of permanent home, with Federal Fiscal Court (BFH) references):
> "If the person has a permanent home in only one state, they are deemed to be resident only in that state. A permanent home means premises which, by their nature and furnishing, are suitable for living, which can be used permanently and which are actually used regularly. A dwelling used as the family home is to be regarded as a permanent home available to the person within the meaning of Art. 4(2) OECD-MA until the end of the tenancy or the family's actual move-out. It is a place of contact integrated into the person's general rhythm of life (BFH judgments of 23 October 1985, BStBl II 1986 p. 133, of 16 December 1998, BStBl II 1999 p. 207 and of 5 June 2007, BStBl II p. 812)."

Para. 14 (centre of vital interests, second step):
> "Provided the person has a permanent home in both states, they are deemed to be resident only in the state with which they have the closer personal and economic relations (centre of vital interests). In doing so, the personal and economic relations must be weighed against each other on the basis of objective indications of the individual case (BFH judgment of 31 October 1990, BStBl II 1991 p. 562)."

Para. 18 (key rule of thumb on time limits, directly quotable for the workation context):
> "In the case of a fixed-term posting of five years or more, there is a rebuttable presumption that a person's weightier personal relations shift to the state of posting. In the case of a posting of up to one year, by contrast, there is a rebuttable presumption that a person's personal relations remain in the state of the retained home."

Example 2 (para. 19, very close to the workation case — fixed-term stay abroad of up to 1 year, dwelling in Germany is kept, employment relationship continues):
> "The centre of vital interests - irrespective of marital status - as a rule remains in Germany (BFH judgment of 23 October 1985, BStBl II p. 133), since in the case of a stay in the state of activity limited to one year, with the continuation of the existing employment relationship and the retention of the existing dwelling in Germany, the personal and economic relations to Germany are usually weightier than those to the other state."

Para. 24 (cascade to steps 3-5):
> "If residence cannot be determined on the basis of the permanent home, because a person has no permanent home in either contracting state or the centre of vital interests cannot be determined, the habitual abode is to be used first and nationality last. If residence cannot be determined even by the latter criterion, because the person holds the nationality of both states or of neither state, the states concerned settle residence by mutual agreement."

BFH judgments cited by the BMF circular for the definition of the permanent home (for footnotes in the article):
- BFH judgment of 23.10.1985, BStBl II 1986 p. 133
- BFH judgment of 16.12.1998, BStBl II 1999 p. 207
- BFH judgment of 5.6.2007, BStBl II p. 812
- BFH judgment of 31.10.1990, BStBl II 1991 p. 562 (centre of vital interests, weighting)

## Source 3 — Haufe Personal/Steuer, "Praxis-Beispiele: Auslandstätigkeit / 26 Workation" (practical examples: work abroad / 26 workation)
URL: https://www.haufe.de/id/beitrag/praxis-beispiele-auslandstaetigkeit-26-workation-HI15506481.html
Retrieved: 2026-09-05

Practical example (facts): employee A, resident in Germany and subject to unlimited tax liability, spends 3 months in winter in a "warmer state W" and continues to work remotely for his German employer G — the residence in Germany is maintained, no residence/habitual abode is established in state W.
> Quote (translated from German): "For the application of the DBA, Germany is the state of residence, since A has his residence here and is subject to unlimited tax liability."

Confirmed in professional practice: in a classic workation (several weeks to months) without a permanent home established abroad, Germany remains the state of residence under Art. 4(2)(a) OECD-MA — the tie-breaker examination already ends at step 1.

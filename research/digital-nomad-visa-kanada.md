## Key message
Canada has NO dedicated "Digital Nomad Visa". Remote workers enter as ordinary visitors (eTA/TRV); the business visitor exemption R186(a) permits work for a foreign-based business without a Canadian work visa under narrow conditions. German social security: a German-Canadian social security agreement (Sozialversicherungsabkommen, SVA-Kanada) exists; Ausstrahlung/section 4 of the Social Code Book IV (§ 4 SGB IV — continued application of German social security law during temporary work abroad) must be assessed separately, no automatic protection for remote work without a formal posting (Entsendung). For tax, the 183-day sojourner rule (CRA) applies, regardless of the residence title.

## Sources (all retrieved and read, 2026-09-02)

### 1. IRCC — Business visitors [R186(a)] (official, canada.ca)
URL: https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/corporate/publications-manuals/operational-bulletins-manuals/temporary-residents/foreign-workers/work-without-permit/business.html
Quote: "The business visitor work permit exemption under paragraph 186(a) of the Immigration and Refugee Protection Regulations (IRPR) facilitates entry to Canada for foreign nationals who intend to engage in international business activities in Canada. These activities are considered to be work... even though they are not directly entering the Canadian labour market."
Criteria (General criteria): "The foreign national is not directly entering the Canadian labour market if... the primary source of the remuneration for the business activity remains outside Canada [and] the principal place of business of the foreign national is located outside Canada... The business activity of the foreign national is international in scope."
→ No dedicated "Digital Nomad" programme; remote work for a foreign employer can fall under this exemption, but it is a case-by-case decision by the border officer, not a blanket permission.

### 2. IRCC — Electronic Travel Authorization (eTA), official
URL: https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/visit-canada/eta/about.html
Quote: "With a valid eTA, you can travel to Canada as often as you want for short stays (normally for up to 6 months at a time)." and "An eTA doesn't let you work or study in Canada."
→ Stay normally max. 6 months per entry; the eTA itself does not permit work — the R186(a) exemption must additionally apply.

### 3. CRA — Income Tax Folio S5-F1-C1 "Determining an Individual's Residence Status" (official, canada.ca)
URL: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/technical-information/income-tax/income-tax-folios-index/series-5-international-residency/folio-1-residency/income-tax-folio-s5-f1-c1-determining-individual-s-residence-status.html
Quote (¶1.32): "An individual who has not established sufficient residential ties with Canada... but who sojourns... in Canada for a total of 183 days or more in any calendar year, is deemed to be resident in Canada for the entire year, under paragraph 250(1)(a)." (¶1.33): "The CRA considers any part of a day to be a day for the purpose of determining the number of days."
→ From 183 days, tax liability on worldwide income in Canada, regardless of residence title/eTA period.

### 4. DVKA — German-Canadian Agreement on Social Security (Abkommen über Soziale Sicherheit) (official, GKV-Spitzenverband – National Association of Statutory Health Insurance Funds)
URL: https://www.dvka.de/de/arbeitgeber-erwerbstaetige/laenderspezifische-informationen/abkommenstaaten/kanada/abkommen.html
Confirmed: an official "Agreement on Social Security" (Abkommen über Soziale Sicherheit) between Germany and Canada exists (PDF documents: agreement, final protocol, implementing arrangement), including a reference to the "posting abroad" (Entsendung ins Ausland) procedure in the context of the agreement.

### 5. Deutsche Rentenversicherung (German pension insurance, DRV) — overview of social security agreements (official)
URL: https://www.deutsche-rentenversicherung.de/DRV/DE/Rente/Ausland/Sozialversicherungsabkommen/sozialversicherungsabkommen_detailseite.html
Quote (translated from German): the list of existing agreements explicitly names "Kanada und Quebec" (Canada and Quebec) among the bilateral social security agreements (alongside Australia, India, USA etc.), which "govern the acquisition of pension entitlements and the payment of pensions to the respective country".

### 6. rvRecht (Deutsche Rentenversicherung legal portal) — SVA-Kanada Art. 1
URL: https://rvrecht.deutsche-rentenversicherung.de/SharedDocs/rvRecht/02_GRA_EU_SVA/04_Laender/Kanada/SVA_Kanada/gra_kanada_sva_a_0001.html
Confirms the existence + structure of the SVA-Kanada in the DRV's official legal portal (definitions, insurance periods) — primary source for the legal classification that Canada is NOT covered via the A1 certificate (A1-Bescheinigung) (EU/EEA/CH only), but via the bilateral agreement (Certificate of Coverage equivalent).

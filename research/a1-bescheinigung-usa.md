## Core topic
The A1 certificate (A1-Bescheinigung) applies only within the EU/EEA/Switzerland (Regulation (EC) No 883/2004). For postings (Entsendungen) to the USA — not an EU/EEA/CH state — the bilateral German-American social security agreement (Sozialversicherungsabkommen; Totalization Agreement) of 1976 applies instead, with the country-specific proof form **D/USA 101** (Certificate of Coverage). premote so far covers the topic only in general terms via the articles "Sozialversicherungsabkommen" and "Certificate of Coverage", without a country-specific USA-focused article — a genuine content gap.

## Sources (all retrieved and read in the session fetch, 2026-08-28)

### Source 1 — SSA.gov (official US authority, retrieved via Wayback Machine snapshot because ssa.gov blocks live fetches with 403/Access Denied)
URL: https://www.ssa.gov/international/Agreement_Pamphlets/germany.html (Snapshot: http://web.archive.org/web/20240121052154id_/https://www.ssa.gov/international/Agreement_Pamphlets/germany.html)
Quote: "To establish your exemption from coverage under the U.S. Social Security system, your employer in Germany must request a certificate of coverage (form D/USA 101) from the local German Sickness Fund that collects your social security taxes in Germany."
Quote: "Self-employed workers ... who transfer their trade or business to the other country for five years or fewer will remain covered under the country from which the worker transferred the self-employment activity."
Facts: In Germany, the agreement covers: Wage Earners' Pension Insurance, Salaried Employees' Pension Insurance, Miners' Pension Insurance, Steelworkers' Supplementary Pension Insurance, Farmers' Old-Age Security, as well as contributions that finance German health and long-term care insurance (in the sense of the agreement's contribution exemption, NOT in the sense of substantive benefit coverage of these branches — see Source 3). Self-employed: limit of 5 years (five years or less) for retaining home-country status.

### Source 2 — Deutsche Rentenversicherung (German pension insurance, DRV), official German authority (quotes translated from the German original)
URL: https://www.deutsche-rentenversicherung.de/DRV/DE/Rente/Ausland/Ansprechpartner-und-Verbindungsstellen/USA/USA.html
Quote: "If you work in the USA only temporarily for your German employer and continue to be paid by them, you can remain subject to compulsory insurance in Germany. However, this so-called posting is limited to 60 months. For this, as an employee, self-employed person or civil servant, you need a certificate of coverage (Entsendebescheinigung) D/USA 101."
Quote: "Your employer applies for the certificate of coverage at the statutory health insurance fund to which your pension insurance contributions are paid. If you are not subject to compulsory pension insurance, Deutsche Rentenversicherung Bund issues the certificate of coverage."
Quote: "If it is clear from the outset that the employment will last longer than 60 months, or if for other reasons it does not constitute a posting, you and your employer can apply for a so-called exception agreement (Ausnahmevereinbarung) in order to remain insured in Germany. For this, contact the Deutsche Verbindungsstelle Krankenversicherung - Ausland (DVKA)."
Quote: "The German-US social security agreement contains only provisions on pension insurance. All other areas of social security are not covered."
Facts: Maximum posting duration 60 months (5 years); competent body = statutory health insurance fund (collection agency for pension insurance contributions) or DRV Bund (if no compulsory pension insurance); exception agreement via the DVKA for assignments foreseeably lasting longer; the agreement covers ONLY pension insurance — health, unemployment and long-term care insurance are not covered (important difference from the EU A1, which covers all branches).

### Source 3 — Deutsche Rentenversicherung, legal portal rvRecht (official implementing instruction/administrative agreement text, separate legal reference database) (quotes translated from the German original)
URL: https://rvrecht.deutsche-rentenversicherung.de/SharedDocs/rvRecht/02_GRA_EU_SVA/04_Laender/USA/DV_zum_SVA_USA/gra_usa_sva_usa_dv_uebersicht.html
Quote: "Agreement between the Federal Republic of Germany and the United States of America on Social Security of 07.01.1976 (SVA-USA). The agreement governs the pension-law relations between the two contracting states."
Quote: "Agreement on administrative measures of 04.03.2004 for the implementation of the agreement of 07.01.1976 (VV zum SVA-USA)."
Facts: Confirms the exact date of the agreement (07.01.1976) and the implementing/administrative agreement (2004) as the legal basis for procedures, competences and forms including D/USA 101.

### Source 4 (independent, third organisational sphere) — touring-artists.info (specialist portal for international cultural professionals, project funded among others by the German Cultural Council (Deutscher Kulturrat) / BMWK sphere, cites the DVKA as primary source) (quotes translated from the German original)
URL: https://www.touring-artists.info/sozialversicherung/unterwegs-ueber-europa-hinaus/entsendungen-staaten-mit-abkommen
Quote: "Unlike EC law, the bilateral agreements each cover only individual insurance branches (e.g. health insurance, unemployment insurance, etc.). And only for these branches do the provisions of the agreement then apply, with the aim of avoiding double insurance."
Quote: "For these states it is therefore strongly advised to take out private international health insurance before the assignment abroad, which among other things also covers repatriation."
Facts: Confirms the structural difference between bilateral agreements and EU coordination law (only partial areas instead of all branches); recommends private international health insurance for states without comprehensive agreement coverage — directly relevant to the USA case, since health/long-term care insurance are not covered there (consistent with Source 2).

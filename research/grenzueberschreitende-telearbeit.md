## Key facts, verified across ≥2 sources each

- **Legal basis:** Art. 16(1) Regulation (EC) No 883/2004 allows two or more Member States (or their competent authorities/institutions) to agree exceptions to the applicable-legislation rules of Title II, in the interest of certain persons or categories of persons. [DVKA text Art. 1–3; EUR-Lex H14 recitals]
- **Baseline rule without an agreement:** under Art. 13(1)(a) Reg. 883/2004 + Art. 14(8)/(10) Reg. 987/2009, an employee who works in two or more Member States is subject to the social security legislation of the state of residence once cross-border telework there reaches **25% or more** of total working time ("substantial activity" threshold). [Guidance Note AC 137/23, §II; DVKA text Art. 2(2)]
- **What the framework agreement changes:** on request (both employer and employee must consent), the employer's-state legislation can continue to apply instead — **provided cross-border telework in the state of residence stays below 50% of total working time** ("less than 50 % of the total working time", translated from German, DVKA text Art. 3; confirmed identically on the Belgian FPS depository page: "less than 50% of the total working time"). This creates a workable band of **25%–49.99% home-country telework** where the employer can keep the employee in the employer-state system via a joint application, avoiding a forced switch to the residence state's social security system.
- **Scope:** applies only to employed persons (not self-employed) who telework habitually across a border between two signatory states, where one state is the residence state and the other is the sole employer's state. Does not apply to persons who: work in more than the two relevant states, do work other than telework in the residence state, or are self-employed. [DVKA text Art. 2(3)]
- **Procedure:** request follows Art. 18 of Reg. 987/2009; agreements run for a **maximum of 3 years**, renewable on reapplication; competent institution issues a **PD A1** certificate under Art. 19 Reg. 987/2009 confirming the applicable legislation. [DVKA text Art. 4]
- **Retroactivity window:** requests can cover backdated periods of up to 3 months before the application date as a general rule; a wider 12-month retroactive window applied only for applications filed by 30 June 2024 (transitional rule). [DVKA text Art. 4(3)]
- **Entry into force / duration:** the framework agreement entered into force **1 July 2023** for states that had signed by then; requires at least 2 signatory states to take effect; runs for **5 years**, auto-renewing for further 5-year periods unless terminated. A state signing later joins on the first day of the month following its signature. Any signatory can withdraw with 3 months' written notice to the depository state. [DVKA text Art. 6]
- **Depository state:** **Belgium** — collects signed versions, publishes the agreement, and informs all signatory states (Art. 5). Belgium's FPS Social Security publishes and maintains the current signatory list. [DVKA text Art. 5; Belgian FPS page]
- **Signatory states (as published by Belgian FPS depository page, effective dates):** Austria, Belgium, Croatia, Czechia, Finland, France, Germany, Liechtenstein, Luxembourg, Malta, Netherlands, Norway, Poland, Portugal, Slovakia, Slovenia (from 1 Sept 2023), Spain, Sweden, Switzerland — all from **1 July 2023**; Italy from **1 January 2024**; Lithuania from **1 May 2024**; Ireland from **1 June 2024**; Estonia from **1 February 2026**. The **United Kingdom has explicitly stated it will not sign**. Others may still join. This list changes over time — link out to the Belgian FPS page rather than hard-coding a "final" list in evergreen copy.
- **Historical context (COVID-19 transitional guidance):** a force-majeure-based guidance (AC 125/22, extended by AC 125/22REV3) kept pre-pandemic applicable legislation unchanged for teleworkers from Feb 2020 through 30 June 2023, bridging into the new framework agreement from 1 July 2023 so there was no coverage gap for employees already teleworking. [EUR-Lex H14 recitals 1–9; Guidance Note AC 137/23 intro]
- **What happens outside the 25–49.99% band / without an agreement:** below 25% telework in the residence state, the employer-state legislation continues to apply as normal (lex loci laboris / employer-state rule) without needing this mechanism; at 50% or more, the framework agreement's derogation is not available at all — the residence-state legislation applies under the ordinary Art. 13(1)(a) rule, full stop. [DVKA text Art. 3; Guidance Note §II]

## Sources (all fetched/read in this session)

### 1. Deutsche Rentenversicherung (German pension insurance, DRV) — "Arbeiten im Ausland" (Working abroad) (quotes translated from the German original)
https://www.deutsche-rentenversicherung.de/DRV/DE/Rente/Ausland/Arbeiten-im-Ausland/arbeiten-im-ausland_node.html
Quote: "For postings to states with which Germany has concluded a social security agreement,
you do not need an A1 form, but other certificates of coverage. For all
other countries (for example Mexico or Indonesia), no certificates of coverage
are required." Also explains "non-treaty countries" (vertragsloses Ausland) → possible double insurance ("Ausstrahlung", extension of German social security law abroad).
"The bilateral certificate on the applicable legislation („Entsende-Bescheinigung“, posting certificate)
serves as proof."

### 2. Deutsche Rentenversicherung — "Antragsformulare für Entsendebescheinigungen auf Basis
bilateraler Sozialversicherungsabkommen" (Application forms for certificates of coverage based on bilateral social security agreements)
https://www.deutsche-rentenversicherung.de/DRV/DE/Rente/Ausland/Ansprechpartner-und-Verbindungsstellen/entsendebescheinigung
Key facts (primary source, complete country competence table):
- Competence is NOT determined by statutory/private health insurance (GKV/PKV) (unlike the A1), but by where the
  pension insurance contributions flow: to a health insurance fund (as collection agency) or not.
- If pension insurance contributions are paid to a health insurance fund: since 1 January 2026, application exclusively
  electronically via the SV-Meldeportal (app.sv-meldeportal.de).
- If no pension insurance contributions are paid to a health insurance fund (e.g. civil servants, self-employed,
  persons exempt from compulsory insurance such as lawyers/doctors/architects): competence according to the table
  is either the DVKA (German liaison office for health insurance abroad) in Bonn or Deutsche Rentenversicherung Bund, differing by destination country.
- Country table (excerpt): USA → DVKA; Canada, Japan, Korea, China, India, Brazil, Chile,
  Australia, Philippines, Uruguay, North Macedonia, Albania → DRV Bund; Turkey, Bosnia-Herzegovina,
  Israel, Kosovo, Morocco, Moldova, Montenegro, Serbia, Tunisia → DVKA. Switzerland: this rule applies only to
  third-country nationals (e.g. Americans, Japanese) — for EU/EEA citizens in
  Switzerland the regular A1 certificate applies instead.
- Each country has its own form code, e.g. Canada: "CAN 1", Japan: "J/D 101", Korea: "K/D 101",
  China: "VRC/DE 101", Australia: "AU/DE 101", Brazil: "BR/DE 101".
- The forms simultaneously check for a "posting within the meaning of Ausstrahlung (§ 4 SGB IV)".

### 3. § 4 SGB IV (Ausstrahlung) — statutory text
https://dejure.org/gesetze/SGB_IV/4.html
Quote para. 1 (translated from German): "Insofar as the provisions on compulsory insurance and
entitlement to insurance presuppose employment, they also apply to persons who, within the
framework of an employment relationship existing within the territorial scope of this Code, are posted to a
territory outside this scope, if the posting is limited in time in advance as a result of the nature
of the employment or by contract." Para. 2: applies accordingly to
the self-employed. This is the statutory basis on which the certificate of coverage (Entsendebescheinigung) /
Certificate of Coverage in non-EU agreement states rests (parallel to Art. 12 Regulation (EC) No 883/2004 for the
A1 certificate in the EU context).

### 4. Switzerland: EAK (Eidgenössische Ausgleichskasse, Swiss Federal Compensation Office) — "Bescheinigung A1, Certificate of Coverage (CoC)" (quotes translated from the German original)
https://www.eak.admin.ch/de/bescheinigung-a1-certificate-of-coverage-coc
Quote: "With a posting certificate (A1, CoC), the employee can prove to foreign
authorities that Swiss social security law applies to them during their
assignment abroad." Important practical note: "Otherwise, for very short, one-off
assignments abroad (business trips, seminars, participation in exhibitions and trade fairs), applying for the
posting certificate can be dispensed with for reasons of proportionality." Shows: the
CoC concept is not Germany-specific but internationally common (Certificate of Coverage
is the internationally used, especially Anglo-American, term for the same
instrument).

### 5. Netherlands: business.gov.nl — "A1 certificate of coverage for social security"
https://business.gov.nl/regulations/a1-certificate-coverage/
Quote: "An A1 certificate is a document that states in which country a worker's social insurance
premiums are paid. Without an A1 certificate you or your employee cannot work in some countries."
Confirms: A1 = EU counterpart, "certificate of coverage" is the internationally common
umbrella term under which the A1 form is also often marketed/explained — the reason for the
terminological confusion that drives the SERP traffic for "certificate of coverage" (many users search
with the English umbrella term but, depending on the destination country, mean the A1 or a bilateral
certificate of coverage).

## Editorial implications (distinction from the article "sozialversicherungsabkommen")
The existing article explains social security agreements in general, but does not concretely name the
document that is issued. This supplement/new article closes the gap: clear clarification of terms and
competences (health insurance fund vs. DVKA vs. DRV Bund), example table with countries/form codes,
distinction from the A1 certificate, and a pointer to the SV-Meldeportal (electronic application from
1.1.2026). Direct Competitor A gap: their glossary page ranks #4 for "certificate of coverage", premote
has nothing on it.

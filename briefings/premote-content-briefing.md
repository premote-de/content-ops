## 1. Positioning

premote is the all-in-one Global Mobility platform for every form of mobility, from the single business trip to the multi-year assignment (Entsendung). The compliance behind it runs automatically out of the HR and travel system, not as a consulting project.

In scope: business travel, postings and assignments (Entsendungen), workation, travel risk management, Assignment Management (Cost Projections, permanent establishment reporting, risk and compliance assessments), tax and permanent establishment (Betriebsstätte) data as evidence and reporting.

Not in scope: EOR, payroll processing, relocation and moving logistics, immigration as legal advice.

Lead persona: Global Mobility Managers and heads of HR in companies with their own GM function, with a focus on plant engineering, mechanical engineering, renewables and project business. Second track: HR in the Mittelstand (mid-sized companies) without a GM team of its own.

## 5. Cluster C: Matrix Compliance and global roles

This is the replacement for the previous cost cluster and at the same time the largest and most important block. The old scoping was too abstract. The core is not calculation but the question of which cross-border management, role or cost constellation triggers which obligation, often without anyone having been physically posted.

Market situation: the topic has been on the agenda of every Global Mobility conference for around two years and nobody solves it cleanly. Subject-matter understanding at a high level exists in the market; the technical implementation is missing everywhere. premote is building an assessment module for it, release towards the end of the year. Content can build demand here before the product arrives.

Two rules for the whole cluster: first, all figures in this area are practice benchmarks, not legal norms, see section 9. Second, every article here needs a note that it does not constitute legal or tax advice.

### C1: Fundamentals and delineation

Well founded in substance. This is the level at which practically nothing usable exists on the German web.

19. What is a matrix structure, and at what point does it become a compliance issue?
20. Matrix, remote work, posting or purely local case. Distinguishing the four categories
21. Disciplinary or functional line manager. The difference that counts for tax purposes
22. Am I allowed to manage an employee across the national border in a disciplinary capacity?
23. Privately motivated or company-initiated. The question that decides the permanent establishment risk
24. Matrix Role or Group Role. Why the specific role assignment is a project in its own right
25. When does a new hire have to go through a cross-border assessment at all? The pre-filter in five questions
26. Employee lives abroad and serves the local market from there. Privately or business-motivated?
27. Place of work and country of residence diverge. What applies then
28. Employment country or country of residence. Which country counts for the assessment

Substance for this block: the basic typology distinguishes matrix framework (business-driven), remote work (privately motivated), posting and purely local role. The rule of thumb on management is that the disciplinary line manager sits in the same country as the employee; the functional one may sit abroad. Cross-border disciplinary management is problematic under employment law and suboptimal for tax purposes, but is done frequently in practice. For assessing the permanent establishment risk, the decisive factor these days is whether commercial reasons lie behind it or whether the constellation is purely privately motivated. The pre-filter checks: does the country of employment differ from the country of work or residence, does the disciplinary or functional line manager sit abroad, do direct reports sit abroad, is there a statutory officer position (Organstellung) at another country entity, is any form of management, responsibility or value creation across the border intended. If the answer to everything is no, it is a local case.

### C2: The cluster model for the economic employer (wirtschaftlicher Arbeitgeber)

The most original block. The model does not exist in this form on the German web.

29. Clustering matrix cases instead of assessing them individually. The cluster model at a glance
30. The Statthalter ("figurehead") cluster. When the manager in his own country is not allowed to decide anything
31. Benefit or Non Benefit. What the domestic company actually gets out of a management role abroad
32. Replacement. Why the successor abroad is the riskiest matrix setup
33. 80 out of 100 cases via clusters, 20 to the tax advisor. The realistic division of labour
34. Why a traffic-light system is not enough for matrix cases
35. Who actually approves leave? The underrated indicator for the economic employer
36. Parameters without weighting. Why the Federal Ministry of Finance circular (BMF-Schreiben) does not say at what point it becomes critical
37. Gewichtungsauskunft ("weighting ruling"). Securing the assessment method with the tax office instead of the individual case

Substance: four clusters, Statthalter, Benefit, Non Benefit and Replacement. Statthalter means the person in the home country holds the role formally, cannot decide anything, and is steered from abroad. Benefit and Non Benefit distinguish whether the cross-border management role actually brings a benefit to the domestic company or exists only on paper. Replacement is the sharpest criterion of all: a domestic manager is replaced by a foreign one, and setup, tasks and the team being managed remain identical. The clusters are similar across companies because organisational structures are rarely truly individual. Practical rule: roughly 80 percent of cases can be handled via clusters, the rest goes into individual review. The test parameters themselves come from the BMF circular on the economic employer, but are not weighted there, which is why an overall assessment of the circumstances is always necessary. Beyond the circular, four further dimensions are worth including: strength of the reporting line, content of the activity, travel frequency and span of control.

### C3: Economic employer and the legal consequences

The block with the highest stakes. Needs legal backing throughout, but in return it holds every reader who carries responsibility.

38. Economic employer. When tax liability arises from the first day of travel
39. How many days a year may I travel abroad to my team?
40. Cross-charge. Why costs have to follow substance, in both directions
41. Only one shot. Disclosure, voluntary self-disclosure with release from liability (haftungsbefreiende Selbstanzeige), or criminal tax proceedings
42. Why an advance ruling under section 42e Income Tax Act (Anrufungsauskunft nach § 42e EStG) at the wrong moment puts a whole project under time pressure
43. Blocking effect of the external wage tax audit (Lohnsteueraußenprüfung). Why timing decides everything
44. Managing director liability for undetected matrix cases
45. Reporting without undue delay with a hundred legacy cases. The contradiction between law and practice
46. Daily or monthly wage tax table (Tagestabelle oder Monatstabelle). Why a matrix case costs more today than five years ago

Substance: if an employee is economically borne by another company and travels there, tax liability arises there from the first day of travel, not only after a day threshold. For travel days to team and line managers there is no legal basis, only a weighing of risk; in practice 24 days a year are treated as uncritical and 60 days as clearly risky. Cross-charge is checked in both directions; neither recharging without substance in the destination country nor substance in the destination country without recharging is consistent. If a legacy stock of such cases is discovered, the path leads either into a voluntary self-disclosure with release from liability or into criminal tax proceedings, in the worst case retroactively for ten years, and the disclosure has to be complete. An incomplete clean-up is worse than none. An announced or ongoing external wage tax audit blocks the path to voluntary self-disclosure. Taxation under the daily table and the loss of the basic personal allowance in these cases make it worse.

### C4: Permanent establishment, powers of attorney and cost recharging

47. Home office abroad. When it turns into a permanent establishment
48. Dependent agent PE (Vertreterbetriebsstätte) through Prokura (registered commercial power of attorney). When the home office becomes a risk
49. Prokura, Handlungsvollmacht (general commercial authority), Einzelvollmacht (specific power of attorney), Organstellung (statutory officer position). Which powers of attorney belong in the assessment
50. Functional Steering Role or value-creating global role. The difference decides the cost recharging
51. When do I need a Service Level Agreement for a global role?
52. Travelling as a service provider of the parent. How an SLA prevents a change of economic employer
53. I sit in Munich and work for a French project, all costs go to France. Do I need a posting?
54. Customer site as a permanent establishment risk. When employees sit at the customer's premises permanently

Substance: the role typology distinguishes the Functional Steering Role, which fulfils an overarching governance task purely in the shareholder's interest and whose costs are therefore not recharged, from the global or regional role with a value-creating function for subsidiaries, whose costs are recharged into the group via a Service Level Agreement with allocations and mark-up. The SLA at the same time acts as mitigation: the person then travels not as an employee of the visited company but as a service provider of the parent company, so the visited company does not become the economic employer. Powers of attorney are the most frequently overlooked source of risk; four types plus statutory officer positions are relevant, and in addition always the question of which company in which country the power of attorney applies to. Power of attorney plus a high share of activity in the country of the power of attorney results in the dependent agent PE risk. Permanent activity at a customer site over months or years can also constitute a permanent establishment.

### C5: Social security in cross-border roles

The skeleton is in place; check all figures externally, see section 9.

55. 100 percent home office abroad. When social security mandatorily switches
56. Multistate worker. How much home office the EU rules allow
57. Multilateral framework agreement on cross-border telework (Rahmenvereinbarung Telearbeit). Which countries have signed, and what destroys its applicability
58. Cross-border commuters (Grenzgänger). Lives abroad, commutes daily, and why that is the simplest case

Substance: with the entire activity performed in the home office in the country of residence, social security mandatorily switches; the employer must register there and pay contributions. With split activity, the multistate worker rule applies, and it must then be checked whether the state concerned has signed the multilateral framework agreement on cross-border telework. Important practical note: additional business trips to further states can destroy the applicability of this framework agreement. Someone who lives abroad but commutes daily to the employer's state is the unproblematic case, because employer state and state of activity coincide. Caution with terminology: it is a framework agreement, not a directive.

### C6: Mitigation and measures

This is the part that turns explanatory articles into action articles, and the part that the Big 4 PDFs never deliver.

59. Do's and don'ts for employees abroad. What concretely belongs in them
60. The catalogue of measures. Reduce home office, cap travel days, withdraw power of attorney, set up an SLA
61. Local employment instead of a home office construct. And why the activity has to match the business purpose of the local company
62. Employer of Record. Does it really solve the permanent establishment risk?
63. Proactively registering a permanent establishment or setting up a company. When that is the right answer

Substance: concrete don'ts are no office with a company sign in the destination country, no local telephone number, no local email address, no publicly accessible location. Further measures are reducing home office days, capping travel days, withdrawing the Prokura or power of attorney, local employment, Employer of Record, proactive registration of a permanent establishment or setting up a company, plus the SLA. On the EoR, a deliberately contrarian position that carries well as an article: an EoR does not reduce the permanent establishment risk, it simplifies administration, and the more artificial a construct becomes, the more risk it contains. With local employment the underrated point is that the activity has to match the business purpose of the receiving company.

## 8. What must not be published

- **Stop naming [former customer, name removed] as a reference.** No longer a customer. Please also remove from existing materials.
- **Customer names only in connection with approved statements.** The permanent establishment case from Cluster A comes from an identifiable customer who takes a position in it that is open to challenge for tax purposes. Use only anonymised, for instance as "a project developer in the renewables sector".
- **No personal names.** Neither customer employees nor consultants, tax advisors or partners.
- **No verbatim customer quotes without the customer's approval.**
- **Do not describe identifiable individuals at authorities,** not even without a name via location and specialisation.
- **Project experience only anonymised and disguised.** For clean-up and disclosure projects, namelessness is not enough: the combination of industry, countries involved, number of cases and project duration makes a company reconstructible. Disguise at least industry, countries and number of cases.
- **No criticism of Big 4 firms or other consultancies by name.** The positioning against the old model works without names and without disparagement, and is stronger that way.
- **[customer, name removed] and the permanent establishment module.** The go-live of this module is 1 October 2026. Wording such as "in use for years" would be wrong for this module. For the period before that, "in implementation" applies.
- **Figures only after approval.** In particular any figure on posting volume per customer.
- **No criticism of third-party providers,** for instance of booking or integration systems.
- **Do not claim that the assessment replaces the expert review of the individual case.** Write the limit into every article: the risk dimensions can be automated, the company-specific role definition and the recommendations that follow from it cannot. That is not a weakness but the reason why experts believe us.

## 9. Numbers and legal sources that must be checked before publication

All of the following values are practice benchmarks and experience values, not legal norms. They may only be published after checking against the primary source.

- **Austria, gross salary threshold in the notification.** Internally we work with approx. 7,000 euros; the figure is not substantiated. Check against the Austrian Anti-Wage and Social Dumping Act (LSD-BG) itself and substantiate with date and reference.
- **24 and 60 travel days per year** to team or line managers. Expressly a risk assessment without legal basis. That is precisely the interesting part of the article and has to be said that way.
- **Social security, thresholds.** The percentages used internally are imprecise. Research the relevant values for multistate workers and for the multilateral framework agreement on cross-border telework in full ourselves, including the list of signatories and its date.
- **BMF circular on the economic employer.** Add reference and date and take the complete parameter list from the original circular, not from second-hand sources.
- **Daily table or monthly table.** The current state of this dispute is not certain internally. Research in full ourselves.
- **Gewichtungsauskunft.** The idea of securing not the individual case but the weighting model via an advance ruling under section 42e Income Tax Act (Anrufungsauskunft nach § 42e EStG) is the most original thought in the whole cluster. Have it assessed under tax law before publication.
- **Permanent establishment time limits and country thresholds** from Clusters A and B. Check all values against the respective national source.

Ground rule for the whole track: one wrong figure in a compliance article costs more trust than the article brings reach. When in doubt, leave the figure out and explain the mechanics.

## 10. Terminology and pitfalls

- **"Entsenderichtlinie"** in German search behaviour almost always means the EU Posting of Workers Directive (Entsenderichtlinie), not the internal policy. For the policy topic use "Assignment Policy" or "interne Entsendungsrichtlinie", otherwise two search intents get mixed.
- In everyday usage customers say **"A-Scheine"**, **"EU-Meldungen"** and **"zivilrechtlicher Arbeitgeber"** (civil-law employer). For SEO use the established terms (A1-Bescheinigung, PWD-Meldung, wirtschaftlicher Arbeitgeber, i.e. A1 certificate, PWD notification, economic employer), but pick up the customer words in subheadings and examples. That is exactly the language people use when asking ChatGPT and Perplexity.
- **Project number schemes** (construction project versus service project) are customer-internal. Generalise the principle, do not adopt the nomenclature.
- **"Betriebsstätte" versus "PE"**: in expert conversations practitioners say PE. Use the German term in headings, introduce PE in the body as a synonym so that both search variants are served.
- **Avoid "Remote Work" in Cluster C.** The term is doubly occupied in the market; many understand it to mean workation. For the cross-border employment case, "Cross-Border Employment" or naming the concrete constellation is better.
- **"Statthalter", "Benefit-Cluster", "Gewichtungsauskunft"** are our own coinages, not established technical terms. Strong in substance and usable as a model of our own, but in the article introduce them as a model, not as accepted terminology.
- **Framework agreement, not directive.** The multilateral agreement on telework is often wrongly referred to as a directive in everyday usage.

## Content

A "posted worker" is an employee sent by employer to carry out service in another EU Member State on a temporary basis (contract of services, intra-group posting, hiring via temporary agency).

**Employment Rights:** EU law defines mandatory rules regarding terms and conditions of employment for posted workers to guarantee protection and level-playing field.

**Posting of Workers Directive (PWD):** Adopted 1996, revised 2018. Core terms: remuneration (including overtime rates), maximum work periods, minimum rest periods, minimum paid annual leave, hiring conditions for temporary workers, health/safety, protective measures (pregnant women, children, young people), equal treatment, worker accommodation, travel/board/lodging allowances.

**Key rule:** Where posting exceeds 12 months (or 18 months with notification), all host Member State's applicable terms and conditions of employment must be guaranteed. Exceptions: procedures/formalities of employment contract conclusion and termination (including non-competition), supplementary occupational retirement pension schemes.

**Enforcement:** Enforcement Directive (2014) strengthens PWD application via access to information, inspections/monitoring, joint liability in subcontracting chains, Member State information exchange.

**European Labour Authority (ELA):** Established July 2019 to help Member States and EC ensure fair, simple, effective enforcement of EU labour mobility and social security coordination rules.

**Member States:** Each has single national website on posting with specific terms and obligations, plus national liaison offices and competent authorities.

**Social security:** Regulated through Regulation 883/2004 on coordination of social security systems.

**Road transport:** New rules on posting of drivers (Directive 2020/1057) applicable from 2 Feb 2022; applies to drivers employed by undertakings in Member State posted to another Member State in framework of services contract (transit, bilateral, cross-trade, cabotage operations). Drivers remain under direct authority of original employer.
